Questions and answers
What is the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission, and why was it established?
The U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission (China Commission) was created by Congress to monitor China and the executive branch, especially during the WTO accession and Permanent Normal Trade Relation discussions, as Congress concluded they didn't like the ongoing changes and wanted to keep a close watch.
How does the China Commission differentiate its focus from other entities in Washington D.C.?
The China Commission focuses on "over the horizon" issues - more distant economic, military, and technology challenges that Congress should consider for smarter policy - contrasting with "alligators closest to the boat" issues that immediate policymakers handle.
Why is Congressional involvement considered crucial for U.S.-China policy, according to the speakers?
Congressional involvement is crucial because administrations are fleeting, and durable, lasting policies require Congress; many significant U.S.-China policies like sanctions, CHIPS and Science, and CFIUS reform originated from Congress, driving the agenda and providing executive branch tools.
What dynamic arises when the executive branch does not fully implement legislation related to China, such as the Taiwan Relations Act or the TikTok saga?
A "sloshing around" push-pull relationship arises, where Congress, when the executive doesn't listen, finds ways to impose guardrails, constraints, or funding prohibitions, reflecting a historical tension that is never perfectly in balance.
What problem does the proposed "economic statecraft agency" aim to solve, and what is its underlying vision?
The proposed "economic statecraft agency" aims to solve the problem of insufficient coordination and integration among government entities handling sanctions and export controls, consolidate these functions into one silo with a senior leader to force decisions to the top, and free important national security policies from structural disincentives in current departments.
According to Leland, how does the current government structure inherently set up export controls and sanctions to fail, particularly within departments like Commerce and Treasury?
Leland argues that the current structure sets up these policies to fail because, for example, the Commerce Secretary's role in promoting U.S. businesses abroad creates an inherent tension and disincentive for policies like export controls that interfere with that major mandate.
What fundamental issue currently prevents the U.S. from developing effective policies to address supply chain and technology vulnerabilities with China?
The fundamental issue is the U.S. government's refusal to track and collect necessary data on investment, supply chains, and technology, which makes it impossible to fully understand the extent of vulnerabilities and, consequently, to form good, evidence-based policies.
What unexpected connection was highlighted between synthetic biology and the future of U.S. space endeavors in the commission's space chapter?
The space chapter highlighted the vital role of synthetic biology in sustaining life in space, whether in outer space, on the moon, or Mars, emphasizing the need to be thoughtful about this ecosystem and its future technological reliance.
