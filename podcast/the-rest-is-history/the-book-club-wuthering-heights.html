Questions and answers
What is the primary purpose of "The Book Club" podcast?
Each week, the hosts delve into fascinating books, uncovering secret stories, the truth about authors, the historical context, and unraveling the books themselves.
How did the idea for "The Book Club" originate?
Dominic and Tabi often discussed books while working on "The Rest is History." They created a bonus series for "The Rest is History Club" members, which received an overwhelmingly positive reaction, leading to the development of the new show.
What unique dynamic characterizes the hosts' discussions on "The Book Club"?
Dominic describes Tabitha as "ludicrously well-read" but possessing "dreadful judgment," while Tabitha counters that Dominic is "very poorly read" and has "bad takes," creating a fascinating tension.
What benefit does "The Book Club" offer to listeners who haven't read the featured books?
Listeners who haven't read the books can still enjoy the conversation, and by listening, they will appear "incredibly well-read" at dinner parties, as the hosts have done the reading for them.
Why was Emily Bronte given the nickname "the Sphinx of the Moors"?
Critics called her this because she was such an enigma and riddle, with very little known about her inner life, allowing readers to project their own interpretations onto her.
What unconventional aspects characterized the Bronte family household during Emily's upbringing?
Despite being often thought of as isolated, the household was very literary. Their father, Reverend Bronte, was highly intelligent but also eccentric, known for walking around with a loaded gun and restricting his children's diet to prevent entitlement.
What surprising acts of violence are attributed to Emily Bronte, and how do they potentially contradict her image?
Emily once repeatedly punched her beloved dog, Keeper, in the face for muddy paws. This, along with her "high Tory politics" and willingness to set dogs on suffragettes, contrasts with the popular image of her as a free-spirited, feminist icon.
