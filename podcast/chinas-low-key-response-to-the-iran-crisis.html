Questions and answers
Why has China's reaction to the protests and potential U.S. intervention in Iran been significantly more subdued compared to its response to the Venezuelan situation?
China's reaction to Iran has been subdued due to less investment in the relationship, with only pro forma denunciations of U.S. threats and no concrete actions like evacuating citizens. The economic ties are not as strong as often portrayed, making Iran less critical for China compared to Venezuela's immediate regional implications.
What is the reality behind the widely reported $400 billion investment figure between China and Iran?
The $400 billion figure is a complete exaggeration, stemming from poor journalism and anonymously sourced articles, bearing no resemblance to the actual hundreds of millions of dollars China invests annually in Iran, which is far from the billions implied by the figure.
How significant is the threat to China's oil supply if both Venezuelan and Iranian oil exports were cut off, as suggested by some conservative media in the U.S.?
While cutting off Venezuelan and Iranian oil would amount to about 20% of China's imported oil, it wouldn't significantly cripple China financially as it has diverse suppliers like Russia, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, and Brazil, and can also seek new partnerships like with Canada.
What is the primary focus of China's substantial military investment, and does it enable global power projection similar to the U.S.?
China's massive military investment is primarily designed for the Western Pacific, specifically focusing on Taiwan, the first and second island chains, and periphery defense, rather than projecting power into distant regions like the Mediterranean, Persian Gulf, or Latin America.
Despite its subdued diplomatic response, how has China already played a role in the Iranian government's ability to suppress protests?
China has quietly assisted the Iranian government by selling a lot of surveillance technology and drones, along with training kits, which have contributed to the increased technological sophistication and effectiveness of the repression seen in Iran over the last decade.
What does the text suggest about China's overall approach to international relations and its role in challenging the U.S.-backed international order?
China is not aiming to replace or overthrow the U.S.-backed international order through aggressive military means but rather seeks to redefine and alter it fundamentally using economic investment opportunities and alternative diplomatic platforms.
What is China's primary concern regarding the Straits of Hormuz, and how does it plan to address potential disruptions there?
China is very concerned about any potential closure or disruption in the Straits of Hormuz, as 30-40% of its imported energy passes through it. Its primary strategy to address such issues is through diplomatic and economic resources, rather than military intervention.
Given the tensions with Iran, why did China's special envoy for the Middle East, Jai Jun, visit Jerusalem and meet with the Israeli Foreign Minister?
China's visit to Jerusalem and meeting with the Israeli Foreign Minister, even amidst tensions involving Iran, underscores Israel's importance to China's Middle East strategy, not just for bilateral economic ties (like R&D cooperation), but also for maintaining connections with diverse partners in the region.
