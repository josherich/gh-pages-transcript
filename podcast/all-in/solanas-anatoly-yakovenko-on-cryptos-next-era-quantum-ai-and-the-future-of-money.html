Questions and answers
What is Anatoly's "insane vision" for finance in 20 to 50 years, and how does Solana's role as the "execution layer" differentiate it from Ethereum, which he sees as the "settlement layer"?
Anatoly's "insane vision" for future finance involves a single giant ledger and a single computer for every market globally, synchronized at the speed of light (120 milliseconds round-trip). He differentiates Solana from Ethereum by stating that Ethereum is the world's settlement layer, focused on minutes-long settlements, while Solana is the world's execution layer, designed for much faster processing, which he believes is "where all the money is made."
What is the purpose of the Clarity Act, and what personal experience does Anatoly share that highlights the need for such legislation in the US crypto space?
The Clarity Act is a complicated piece of legislation aimed at minimizing the cost and difficulty for founders to launch tokens in the United States. Anatoly illustrates its necessity by sharing his experience of spending $2 million out of a $14 million seed and A round on lawyer fees just to launch a token in the US, representing over 10% of his runway.
Anatoly states that Solana's "true mission" was to get stocks, bonds, treasuries, and real-world assets on-chain. Why, then, did "meme coins and NFTs" take off instead, and what does this reveal about the challenges faced by the crypto industry?
Anatoly explains that bringing real-world assets on-chain proved to be a much harder legal and regulation problem than an engineering one. Meme coins and NFTs took off because anyone in the world could create markets for them, and regulation was slow to catch up, making it "annoying that those are the things that come out instead of your true mission."
How does Anatoly envision building a competitive product to TikTok using crypto, and what is the fundamental difference in the monetization mechanism he highlights?
Anatoly believes a competitive TikTok-like product could be built with crypto if it catches "lightning in a bottle," primarily due to a monetization mechanism different from the ad-based one. He describes creators being associated with a coin that has market cap and traction, allowing coin holders to potentially have equity in the project's performance over time, rather than relying on ads which he believes lead to spam and duplication.
What is Anatoly's "contrary opinion" regarding Visa and MasterCard's position in the transaction market, and which entities does he believe are the most "disruptable"?
Anatoly's "contrary opinion" is that Visa and MasterCard are more technology companies with very low profit margins on gross payment volume (around 10 basis points), which he describes as "vapor." He believes the issuer and receiver banks are the most disruptable pieces in the payment system due to their much larger profit margins, suggesting Visa could be more successful by removing banks and using stablecoin transfers.
As an architect, what does Anatoly believe is the fundamental reason Bitcoin has remained resilient against hacks despite so much at stake?
Anatoly attributes Bitcoin's resilience to its simplicity. He states that it is the "simplest protocol you can build" because its focus is solely on settlement, making it easy to understand from an engineering point of view and robust against attacks. He even calls the Bitcoin Nakamoto implementation "the coolest piece of software written in the last 20 years."
Why is there a significant demand for real-world assets (RWAs) to be brought on-chain in DeFi, and what "free lunch" in finance do they enable?
There is demand for RWAs on-chain because DeFi needs non-correlated assets. If all crypto assets are correlated (like meme coins), the system lacks hedging and risk management becomes useless. RWAs like real estate, bonds, and insurance, which are uncorrelated, enable the "only free lunch in finance," which is true hedging, reducing risk for the entire system when used together in a global, synchronized environment.
