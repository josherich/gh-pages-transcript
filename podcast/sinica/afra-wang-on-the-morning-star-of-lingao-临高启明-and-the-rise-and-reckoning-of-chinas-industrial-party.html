What is the 'Morning Star of Ling Gao' and the 'Industrial Party' worldview associated with it?
The 'Morning Star of Ling Gao' is a collectively written science fiction project, identified as China's most influential, and is associated with the 'Industrial Party' worldview, which explores how China explains its historical lag and what salvation looks like.
How does Afra Wong describe the quality of writing in 'Ling Gao Qi Ming' despite its influence?
Afra Wong describes the writing in 'Ling Gao Qi Ming' as 'poorly written' and 'scrappy,' even using irony in the Wired piece title because she personally dislikes reading it.
Who is the primary audience for 'Ling Gao Qi Ming'?
The primary audience for 'Ling Gao Qi Ming' consists of STEM people, technologists, developers, and programmers, often described as the 'elite class of technologists' who are predominantly male.
What is the 'Sentimental Party' (秦华党) and how does it contrast with the Industrial Party (共业党)?
The 'Sentimental Party' refers to a Chinese online civic space, now largely diminished, that focused on human costs of development, environmental issues, and social welfare, contrasting with the Industrial Party's focus on technological progress and industrialization.
According to the Industrial Party, what is the 'salvation arc' for China, and why is it important to them?
The Industrial Party's 'salvation arc' for China is rooted in industrial capability, believing that 'forging iron and smelting copper' (industrialization) is where true power lies, making China strong enough to avoid historical humiliations and bullying from other nations.
How does Afra Wong describe the gendered nature of 'Ling Gao Qi Ming' and the Industrial Party worldview?
Afra Wong describes 'Ling Gao Qi Ming' as a 'semi-misogynist' and 'pre-feminist cultural product' where progress toward female equality is driven by rational calculations for modernization, not moral concern.
